# Downhill anticipation and manual look-down — 4 November 2026

The user selected suggestions 1 and 3 after reviewing the Ribwood descent recording: anticipate lower ground ahead and permit a manual look-down while standing. `CameraFollow` now scans for the nearest lower walkable surface in the facing/travel direction. The scan starts at six world units and extends with movement speed, capped at 1.6 viewport half-widths. Sprinting can detect a landing just beyond the current view so vertical framing is ready when it comes into view. A wall or rising face blocks the scan. Trigger volumes, the player, dynamic props and non-walkable faces are excluded.

A detected lower landing eases into view before departure. Its world height is retained when stepping off, so the preview does not retract just as the player commits. A rising jump cancels that landing preview; ordinary jump anchoring is retained. This is not an additional universal airborne framing mode. Both preview and camera motion are damped, and the upper safety margin eases with the preview rather than switching at takeoff or landing.

Standing still and holding S, Down Arrow, D-pad Down or right stick down for 0.18 seconds reveals more below. Releasing the control or moving restores the usual framing smoothly. Menus, transitions, cutscenes, camera lock zones, staff-shot aiming, ledge hangs/climbs and an attached thread suppress the preview so their existing controls retain ownership. The preview is capped to retain Qori on screen; it cannot reveal arbitrarily deep drops.

The isolated-save Play fixture checks the real six-unit Ribwood ledge at x≈182, lower landing y≈91.714, a full-speed approach, the actual descent, flat-ground behavior, all three manual input routes, release recovery, movement cancellation, modal input blocking, held staff-shot aiming, and the completed Qvale jump behavior. `extra_playtest.cs` covers a sprint approach and jumping from an active preview. `QA.json` reports final measured results; captures include native 3840×2160 and 1920×1080 renders. Earlier failed test receipts document the corrected aim-input setup and the running-speed timing issue that led to extending the probe distance.

No scene, art, terrain or map is changed. Test saves are isolated and Editor settings are restored. Supported targets remain Windows, PS5, Xbox Series X/S and Switch 2. This work is verified in Windows Unity Editor with virtual keyboard/gamepad input. Player builds, console hardware (including Series S and Switch 2 handheld/docked), physical controllers, frame-time performance and peak memory are not tested here.
